Acknowledgement of Country

What is an Acknowledgement of Country?

An Acknowledgement of Country is a statement made by anyone to show respect for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples as the Traditional Custodians of the land, and to recognise their continuing connection to Country. It's a small but meaningful part of reconciliation in Australia - a way of acknowledging history and showing respect at the start of an event, meeting, or gathering.

How is this different from a Welcome to Country?

The two are often confused, but they're not the same thing:

  • Welcome to Country can only be delivered by a Traditional Owner or Elder of that specific Country - it's their way of formally welcoming visitors onto their land.
  • Acknowledgement of Country can be given by anybody, Indigenous or not, and doesn't require a Traditional Owner to be present. It's a mark of respect anyone can offer.

Finding the Traditional Custodians of a place

Before writing an acknowledgement, find out whose Country your event is on. Some reliable starting points:

  • Reconciliation Australia - Acknowledgement of Country and Welcome to Country
  • AIATSIS - Whose Country am I on?
  • AIATSIS - Map of Indigenous Australia
  • Know Your Country - helps you find the First Nations Country you're on

Note: language group boundaries are not always exact or universally agreed upon - when in doubt, use general terms like "the Traditional Custodians of this land" rather than guessing a specific name.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Being tokenistic - reading a generic line without any real understanding or care behind it.
  • Getting the name wrong - guessing or mispronouncing a language group's name instead of checking a reliable source.
  • Copy-pasting the same wording for every event regardless of location - an acknowledgement should reflect the actual place the event is happening.
  • Confusing it with a Welcome to Country - don't claim to "welcome" people to the land on behalf of Traditional Owners; you can only acknowledge.
  • Treating it as a formality to rush through rather than a genuine gesture of respect.

Writing a meaningful acknowledgement

When creating an event, you can choose between three options:

  1. No Acknowledgement of Country - no statement is shown.
  2. Generic - a standard, respectful acknowledgement is shown automatically.
  3. Enhanced - you name the specific Traditional Custodians of your event's location (using the resources above) and write your own statement in your own words, reflecting genuine understanding rather than a copy-pasted template.

A meaningful acknowledgement is specific to the place, sincere in tone, and offered with genuine respect rather than as a checkbox exercise. Watch Shelley Reys' TEDxSydney talk, "Deliver an Acknowledgement of Country that really means something" for a good introduction to what makes one land well.
